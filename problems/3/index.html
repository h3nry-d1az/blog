If AutG={e}, show that G is abelian and that every non-identity element of G has order 2.

Proposed in "A Friendly Introduction to Group Theory", by Jake Wellens. Exercise 2.6.

We will prove the contrapositive, that is, if either G is not abelian or has an element of order greater than two, it has a nontrivial automorphism.
Consider now the first of the two cases, and let g,h∈G such that gh≠hg; in this case conjugation by g, given by πg(a)=gag−1 constitutes the desired map, since πg(h)=ghg−1≠hgg−1=h=e(h). Otherwise, multiplying the left- and right-hand sides by g would yield gh≠hg. It is indeed an automorphism, and its inverse is precisely given by πg−1.
Now, for the second case we may assume G is abelian, as if this was not the case, the previous example would work again. Take now p such that p2≠e, i.e., p≠p−1. Notice that due to commutativity, the map φ(g)=g−1 is an isomorphism, since φ(gh)=(gh)−1=h−1g−1=g−1h−1=φ(g)φ(h), and has an inverse which is itself. Plugging p into φ finally shows that it differs from the identity automorphism: φ(p)=p−1≠p=e(p), just as we wanted to prove.
