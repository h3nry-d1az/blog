<div class="problem">
Alyssa, Bilal, Constantine, and Daiyu each live in a house that is numbered with a positive integer.
<br>

A four-digit perfect square is formed by writing Alyssa's house number followed by Bilal's house number. 
Another four-digit perfect square is formed by writing Constantine's house number followed Daiyu's house 
number. Constantine's house number is $31$ more than Alyssa's house number, and Daiyu's house number is $31$
more than Bilal's house number. What is each person's house number?
</div>

_Proposed as the [problem of the week #24 of year 2025/26](https://cemc.uwaterloo.ca/sites/default/files/documents/2026/POTWE-25-A-N-24-P.html), by the Centre for Education in Mathematics and Computing of the University of Waterloo._

<div class="solution">
Let $a, b, c, d \in \mathbf N$ be the house numbers of Alyssa, Bilal, Constantine, and Daiyu, respectively. By the statement
$$
\begin{cases}
    c &= a + 31,\\
    d &= b + 31;
\end{cases}
$$
hence, they must all be two-digit numbers, since $c$ and $d$ have at least two digits.
<br>

From this we now impose that their concatenations give perfect squares:
$$
\begin{cases}
    m^2 &= 100 a + b,\\
    n^2 &= 100 c + d,
\end{cases}
\implies n^2 = 100(a + 31) + (b + 31) = m^2 + 31\cdot 101,
$$
where rearranging the terms and factoring the difference of squares yields
$$
    n^2 - m^2 = (n+m)(n-m) = 101\cdot 31.
$$
These kinds of problems are usually set up so that the resulting factors are either prime or the number of cases to check are
minimal; this is one of the first category, and thus follows that
$$
\begin{cases}
    n + m &= 101,\\
    n - m &= 31
\end{cases}
\implies
\begin{cases}
    m &= 35,\\
    n &= 66.
\end{cases}
$$
<br>

Finally, because $35^2 = 1225$ and $66^2 = 4356$, the numbers we seek are
$$
    a = 12, \qquad
    b = 25, \qquad
    c = 43, \qquad
    d = 56.
$$
</div>
