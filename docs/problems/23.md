---
title: "The group automorphisms of Q are fixed by their action on 1."
date: '16 Jul 2026'
---

<div class="problem">
Let $\phi: \QQ \longrightarrow \QQ$ be an automorphism of $(\QQ, +)$. Show that
for any $q \in \QQ$, we have $\phi(q) = q\phi(1)$.
</div>

_Proposed as exercise 4.55 in _[Abstract Algebra: An Introductory Course](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-319-77649-1)_,
by Gregory T. Lee._

<div class="solution">
The proof of this result is very similar to that of <a href="../21/">problem
21</a>.
<br>

Notice that if $q \in \ZZ$ then
$$
\phi(q) = \phi(\underbrace{1 + 1 + \cdots + 1}_{q\text{ times}})
= \underbrace{\phi(1) + \phi(1) + \cdots + \phi(1)}_{q\text{ times}}
= q\phi(1),
$$
whereas if $q = r/s \in \QQ$ it follows that
$$
\begin{align*}
&r\phi(1) = \phi(r) = \phi\underbrace{\left(\frac rs + \frac rs + \cdots + \frac rs\right)}_{s\text{ times}}
= s\phi\left(\frac rs\right) = s\phi(q)\\
\implies &\phi(q) = \frac rs \phi(1) = q\phi(1),
\end{align*}
$$
concluding the proof.
</div>
