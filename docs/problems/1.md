<div class="problem">
A box in the shape of a triangle with side lengths $13$, $14$, and $15$ is being used to
pack a pizza pi. The pizza is in the shape of a circle and just touches each of the
three sides of the triangular box. What is the area of the circle?

[{
ctx.scale = 20

triangle = Polygon.triangle_from_lengths(13, 14, 15, zord=-1)
triangle.rotate((0, 0), -5)
circle, *_ = Circle.triangle_incircle(*triangle.points)

ctx.add(triangle, Fill(WHITE))
ctx.add(circle, Fill(LIGHT_GRAY))
ctx.add(Label(circle.x, circle.y, r"\pi", zord=1))
}]
</div>

_Proposed as the [problem of the week #23 of year 2025/26](https://cemc.uwaterloo.ca/sites/default/files/documents/2026/POTWE-25-A-G-23-P.html), by the Centre for Education in Mathematics and Computing of the University of Waterloo._

<div class="solution">

In the geometry lingo, this $\pi$e is what is called the circle <em>inscribed</em> to the triangle, and
the way to measure its radius &ndash; which is the essential parameter from which we will derive
its area &ndash; is by leveraging precisely this condition.
<br>
For the inexperienced reader, pause and try to convince yourself that such a circle intersects with
its bounding shape forming $90^\circ$-angles on such points; using this result we will now divide
the original box into three smaller ones, which share the property that their height is the radius
we are looking for:

[{
ctx.scale = 30

triangle = Polygon.triangle_from_lengths(13, 14, 15)
triangle.rotate((0, 0), -5)
A, B, C = triangle.points

circle, p1, p2, p3 = Circle.triangle_incircle(*triangle.points)
I = (circle.x, circle.y)
t1 = Polygon(A, I, B, zord=-1)
t2 = Polygon(B, I, C, zord=-1)
t3 = Polygon(C, I, A, zord=-1)

ctx.add(t1, Fill(Color(230, 230, 250))) # Lavender
ctx.add(t2, Fill(Color(255, 255, 240))) # Ivory
ctx.add(t3, Fill(LIGHT_GREEN))
ctx.add(circle)

ctx.add(Path(p1, I))
ctx.add(Path(p2, I))
ctx.add(Path(p3, I))

ctx.add(RAngle(A, p1, I), Fill(WHITE))
ctx.add(RAngle(B, p2, I), Fill(WHITE))
ctx.add(RAngle(C, p3, I), Fill(WHITE))
ctx.add(Label(p1[0] - 0.5, p1[1] + 2, "r", zord=1))
ctx.add(Label(p2[0] - 2, p2[1] - 1.5, "r", zord=1))
ctx.add(Label(p3[0] + 2, p3[1] - 1, "r", zord=1))
}]

With this key insight in mind, we can now relate the area of the big triangle,
to that of the smaller ones, and thus to $r$; that is,
$$
[\triangle ABC] = \frac{1}{2}13 r + \frac{1}{2}14 r + \frac{1}{2}15 r = 21r\quad
\implies\quad r = \frac{[\triangle ABC]}{21}.
$$
<br>

However, we still have not figured out $[\triangle ABC]$, and it is Heron's formula
the one that tells us exactly this. Letting $s$ be half of the perimeter, then
$$
[\triangle ABC] = \sqrt{s(s-13)(s-14)(s-15)} = \sqrt{21\cdot 8 \cdot 7 \cdot 6} = 84,
$$
so finally,
$$
r = \frac{84}{21} = 4 \quad\implies\quad [\pi] = \pi \cdot 4^2 = 16\pi.
$$

</div>
